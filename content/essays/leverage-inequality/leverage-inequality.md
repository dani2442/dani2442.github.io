Sources of inequality: Leverage

A few hundred years ago wages between workers within the same profession and different ones did not differ much. Maybe in some jobs where you need physical strength you saw the difference between men, women and children. It makes sense, the level of skill did not influence the output of the work that much. 

[Even in higher in the hierarchy, the range ]


The invention of the clock allowed the measurement of productivity in Venice/Florence in the 15th century. blabla


During the industrial revolution, we saw the increase use of tools that increase productivity by a great margin. You could use these tools (e.g., sewing machine, copywriter, newspapers, engineers, etc) to leverage your work and increase the output of your work. Henry Ford was one of the first to benfit from the increase power of leverage with his factory of cars, reaching almost 1 car/day for every 10 workers. You could see similar levels in other industries, for instance clothes/fabrics increase productivity by greater margins, trains/buses allowed the movement of many people using fewer hands. 

The next big revolution came from software and the new era of the internet. At the begining, software development was slow and few people new how to use it, but the increase in output is hundred of times greater than any other profession. One of the most interesting examples was WhatsApp, that only relied in a few dozen workers to run a service used by billions of people. The ceiling of productivity (and salaries) has increased dramatically during the last few years. The difference between hiring a mid and great developer with all this leverage has become so distant, salaries have started to reflect this. 

And this gap has not stop increasing. The last few years with the use of AI (copilot, agents, etc), we saw the a freezing of juniors while we observed exorbitant salaries in the top AI labs.
